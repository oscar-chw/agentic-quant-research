# Chapter 5. Fractionally differentiated features, item by item

Chapter 5 is the most purely mathematical of the first five, so almost every item here is a formula with a closed form
behind it. The binomial expansion of the backshift operator, the weight recursion derived from it, the convergence and
sign rules that follow from the ratio of successive weights, the relative weight loss of an expanding window, the
fixed-width cut-off, and the search for the smallest differencing order that passes an augmented Dickey-Fuller test
are each their own row, and each is checked against the book by a hand value, a product formula computed independently
of the recursion the code uses, or a simulation whose answer is known. The book's own numbers are used as tests where
it prints them: the second-difference expansion, the critical value near minus 2.8623, and the claim that a series
fractionally integrated at d is recovered by differencing at d. The chapter's recommendation is measured rather than
adopted - on this market the concatenated bar logits are already stationary - and that result is recorded as its own
item with the numbers that decide it.
