# Chapter 22. High-performance computational intelligence and forecasting technologies, item by item

A guest chapter on the CIFT project at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory: why high-performance computing belongs in
streaming data analysis, how HPC hardware and software differ from the cloud, and six use cases. Almost every
infrastructure claim in it is about a machine this project does not have, so almost every row below is not applicable
with the number from docs/hardware.md that decides it: 1 node against a cluster, 12 cores against 512, 14 GB of
recorded data against 20 TB, a home network against InfiniBand, 0 accelerators against GPU nodes.

What does carry over is not the hardware but what replaces it here, and each row says which:

- MPI across nodes becomes process pools on one node, `src/pmlab/afml/parallel.py` and the 4 workers of
  docs/hardware.md.
- HDF5 becomes day-partitioned zstd Parquet with column and row selection, `src/pmlab/store.py`; the point the section
  makes, that the storage format decides the analysis time, is the same point.
- In situ processing, which discards data in flight to beat slow disks, becomes in-stream feature computation in the
  live engine, while the raw books are kept on purpose because they are the research data and cost 0.7 GB a day
  against 2,867 GB free.
- The financial use cases are the part with real counterparts: VPIN is implemented and computed live per volume bar,
  and minute-clock algorithmic trading is measured in the time domain instead of by a Fourier spectrum.
